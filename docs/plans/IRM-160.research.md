# IRM-160 research: what conveys trust, and Apple's touch guidelines

Read on 2026-10-25.

## Trust

- NN/G, Trustworthiness in Web Design: 4 Credibility Factors (2016): design quality, upfront disclosure, comprehensive and current content, connection to the rest of the web. One serious violation can undo earlier credibility.
- NN/G, Trust or Bust: reliable technology, respectful email, consistent honest behaviour.
- NN/G, error-message guidelines, confirmation dialogs and undo: confirm only serious or irreversible actions with a specific button label; prefer undo.
- Stanford Web Credibility Project, 10 guidelines (B. J. Fogg): verifiability, a real organisation, expertise, honest people, easy contact, professional design, ease of use, updated content, restraint with promotion, no errors.
- Baymard Institute: perceived security is judged by how secure a step looks; 19 % of checkout abandonment is lack of trust.
- Amershi et al., Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction, CHI 2019 (Microsoft), G1–G18. Goal: calibrated, not maximal, trust.
- Google PAIR People + AI Guidebook: Explainability + Trust; Errors + Graceful Failure.
- Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (DSA) art. 25: dark patterns banned on platforms. The planned Digital Fairness Act extends this.
- Lag (2023:254), in force 28 June 2025: EN 301 549 / WCAG AA. PTSFS 2024:6: identification understandable and robust, information at CEFR B2 or simpler.
- BankID accessibility requirements for QR login: at least 5 minutes, at least 10 regenerations, offer more time when 20 seconds or less remain, fullscreen on tap, screen-reader text. Gap filed as IRM-161.

## Apple Human Interface Guidelines

Read from developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/<page>.

- Accessibility: 44×44 pt default control size (28 pt minimum); about 12 pt padding around bezeled elements, 24 pt around borderless ones; text enlargeable to 200 %; 17 pt default, 11 pt minimum; WCAG AA contrast; not colour alone; alternatives to gestures; avoid time-boxed views; Reduce Motion.
- Buttons: 44×44 pt hit region; press state; one or two prominent buttons per view; verb labels; never give a destructive action the primary role.
- Typography: default and minimum sizes; avoid light weights; adapt layout to all Dynamic Type sizes; minimise truncation; stack at large sizes.
- Layout: order by importance; adapt by size class, not device; keep functionality across sizes; respect safe areas.
- Gestures: standard meanings; more than one way to interact; indicate unavailable gestures; don't conflict with system gestures.
- Feedback: status next to the item; warn only for unexpected irreversible data loss; explain why a command can't run.
- Alerts: sparingly; not informational only; not for undoable actions; not at launch; specific titles and button results; Cancel on the leading side.
- Modality: only with a clear benefit; short; title names the task; obvious dismiss; confirm before losing content; no stacked modals.
- Loading: show something at once; keep the app usable; show progress.
- Text fields: label besides placeholder; right keyboard type; validate when sensible; number formatting.
- Motion: purposeful, brief, cancellable; never the only carrier of information.
- Dark Mode: follow the system with no app-level setting; at least 4.5:1 contrast.
- Privacy: request only what is needed, when it is needed, with a clear purpose string; a pre-alert screen has one button that opens the system alert.
- Onboarding: fast, optional, interactive; reasonable defaults.
- Playing haptics: system patterns by meaning, consistent, complementary, sparing, optional.
- App Store Review Guideline 4.2, Minimum Functionality: an app must offer more than a repackaged website.
- WebKit (not HIG): `font: -apple-system-body` follows the iOS text-size setting; inputs under 16 px make iOS zoom on focus.
