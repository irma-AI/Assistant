# IRM-160: Write down what makes the app trustworthy and touch-friendly: trust and Apple touch rules in DESIGN.md

## Why

The app holds a company's money, its books and its BankID login. People decide in seconds whether a screen like that is safe, and one broken promise can undo months of goodwill. DESIGN.md says nothing about trust yet, so every agent building a screen decides on its own. This issue adds a short **Trust** section to DESIGN.md. It is about behaviour and wording, not the visual look, which stays the owner's call.

DESIGN.md is a protected path, so this needs the `ci-change` label (set).

## What conveys trust (research summary)

The research shows that trust is earned in three ways. The product looks competent. It behaves predictably. It is honest about what it is doing, what it costs and what can go wrong. Users judge the first by gut feel within seconds. The other two build or break over time.

The full UX, IX, AI and BankID findings are in the Linear issue and are condensed into DESIGN.md; sources are listed in `IRM-160.research.md`.

## Done when

* DESIGN.md has a **Trust** section of at most \~40 lines: the UX, IX and AI rules above as short imperatives, each with its source.
* The section states the rules are behavioural and doesn't decide the visual look.
* Feature issues that touch money, sending or AI link to the section.
* DESIGN.md has a **Touch** section with Apple's Human Interface Guidelines for touch interfaces, translated to the web code the iOS app runs: 44 pt targets and spacing, safe areas, Dynamic Type, gestures with on-screen alternatives, sheets and alerts, feedback, motion, dark mode, keyboards and permissions. It also covers App Store guideline 4.2 (more than a repackaged website). Each rule names its HIG page, read 2026-10-25.

## Decisions

- The full research lives in the issue; DESIGN.md keeps only the rules as imperatives with sources, so it stays short enough to read before every UI change.
- HIG rules are translated to web terms (CSS px, `env(safe-area-inset-*)`, `-apple-system-body`, `prefers-reduced-motion`) because the iOS app is a Capacitor WebView.
- Visual choices (colours, fonts, the Liquid Glass look) stay out: they are the owner's call.
